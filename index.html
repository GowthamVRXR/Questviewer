01 — Features

What it does

One Unity package in your VR app, one application on the PC. Everything else is automatic.

Real-time video and audio

Hardware H.264/HEVC encoding on the headset and Opus audio, carried by WebRTC. Typically well under 100 ms on a local network.

Zero-configuration discovery

Put the headset on the same Wi-Fi and it appears on the PC. No IP addresses, no pairing codes, no router changes.

Many headsets at once

Watch a whole room in a tile grid or a compact list, then click any one to go fullscreen. Tested against a ten-headset target.

Spectator cameras

Stream the player's own view, or any camera in your scene — a third-person chase cam, a fixed stage camera — and switch between them live.

Quality control from the PC

Change resolution, frame rate and bitrate from the receiver while the stream is running. The headset applies it without dropping the session.

Sessions that heal themselves

Headset sleeps, loses tracking or steps outside the guardian? The stream picks itself back up. No restarting the app on either end.

Kind to the headset

Capture renders only while somebody is actually watching, so idle headsets stay cool and hold their charge.

Your footage stays yours

Video travels directly from headset to PC. It never passes through our servers, because we do not run any.

02 — How it works

How it works

Only discovery uses the network's broadcast or an optional relay. The video itself goes straight from the headset to your PC — that is what keeps latency low and footage private.

  1. Add the SDK

    Import the free Quest Streaming SDK into your Unity project. A setup wizard checks your Android and OpenXR settings and fixes what is wrong.

  2. Build and run

    Deploy your app to the headset as usual. Streaming rides along inside it — there is no second app for users to launch.

  3. Open the receiver

    Run Quest Viewer on your Windows PC. Every headset on the network running your app shows up as a tile.

  4. Click and watch

    Pick a tile for fullscreen, switch cameras, change quality, or record the session to a file.
